This is the thesis I presented for the Bachelor's in Telecommunications Engineering, although both of my theses fall within the field of Machine Learning. Before choosing a topic, I knew I wanted two things:

1. You know I am really into finance (you can tell), so I wanted to do something in this field, especially on the quantitative side. The challenge of volatility calibration was one of the most appealing to me, as it was a great opportunity to learn about the volatility surface, the Greeks, etc.
2. Neural networks came up many times during my Dual Bachelor's, but I rarely took the time to really dig into the maths and inner workings behind them, and this was something I wanted to do as well.

Emilio (my tutor) told me about PINNs, and I found them very interesting. After doing some research, I came up with a topic I found just as interesting: using PINNs for local volatility surface calibration and barrier option pricing.

If you have made it this far, wow, you must really like this topic to be in this remote corner of the internet, thinking about reading the thesis of a random guy. If you decide to go for it, I recommend starting with Chapters 2 (Theoretical Background), 4 (Methodology), 5 (Results) and 6 (Conclusions and Future Work). If you read at least one of them, please let me know! I would be very happy to discuss it with you.

The code behind this thesis is available in the [PINNs Options Pricing](/work/pinns-options-pricing/) project.

Hope you enjoy the read!
